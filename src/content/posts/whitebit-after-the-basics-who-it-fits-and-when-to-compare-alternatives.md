## The four-week scorecard

Score WhiteBIT from 1 to 5 on the categories you actually used: onboarding, funding, execution, withdrawal, security controls, records, support, and total cost. Add notes, not just numbers. “Withdrawal was easy” is less useful than “BTC withdrawal to my hardware wallet arrived in 18 minutes; fee was X; address book worked as expected.”

Weight the categories. A frequent trader may prioritize liquidity and API stability; an occasional buyer may care more about bank funding and self-custody transfers.

## WhiteBIT is a good fit when…

- your country and legal entity are clearly supported;
- the fiat or crypto funding route is reliable and reasonably priced;
- the pairs you use have sufficient liquidity;
- security controls fit your threat model;
- withdrawals to your preferred wallet and network are predictable;
- exports and records are adequate for your reporting needs;
- you can keep the exchange balance within a defined limit.

The platform does not need every market. It needs the markets and rails that serve your repeated job.

## Compare alternatives when…

Recurring friction is data. Compare another platform if your bank route repeatedly fails, the asset or network you need is missing, spreads are poor at your size, withdrawals are confusing, support cannot resolve account issues, or records require too much manual repair.

Also compare when your use changes. A platform that worked for monthly spot purchases may not fit a business treasury, an algorithmic strategy, or institutional custody. Growth changes requirements.

## Do not overvalue unused features

Exchange dashboards compete through abundance: futures, margin, bots, earn products, cards, launchpads, and more. Every feature carries different fees, eligibility, and risk. If you joined to buy and withdraw BTC, a derivatives catalogue should not improve the score.

Unused features can even be negative when they increase distraction or make the interface harder to understand. Reward the platform for the job completed, not the possibilities advertised.

## Primary exchange versus backup exchange

A primary exchange handles the repeated funding and trading route. A backup exists for a specific contingency: banking interruption, missing network, or temporary platform maintenance. The backup should be verified and secured before it is needed, but it does not need a large standing balance.

Two accounts can improve resilience. Five unmanaged accounts produce password, KYC, tax, and phishing sprawl. Keep the smallest set that covers real failure modes.

## Revisit custody as the balance grows

A balance that felt harmless during week one may become significant months later. Review the maximum amount kept on WhiteBIT and move long-term assets to self-custody if you are ready to protect the recovery phrase. Exchange convenience should not silently turn into permanent concentration.

If self-custody is not appropriate, investigate regulated custody options rather than pretending the decision does not exist.

## The exit test

Before declaring WhiteBIT your primary exchange, prove that you can leave: withdraw each important asset on the intended network, export transaction history, revoke API keys, close sessions, and understand the account-closure process. A product is easier to trust when the exit is visible.

This does not assume failure. It prevents lock-in and keeps your operational knowledge current.
