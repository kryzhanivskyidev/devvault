## The core difference: account versus keys

On a CEX, you access an account and the platform controls the wallets behind the service. Password recovery and KYC can help restore access, but withdrawals depend on the platform. On a DEX, your wallet signs transactions directly; there may be no support desk capable of reversing an approval or restoring a lost seed phrase.

“Not your keys” describes counterparty custody risk. “Your keys, your responsibility” describes the operational risk that replaces it.

## CEX strengths and risks

Centralized exchanges can offer bank/card funding, liquid order books, familiar account security, transaction history, and customer support. They are practical entry points for beginners and useful for converting between fiat and crypto.

Risks include platform custody, account freezes, phishing, policy changes, insolvency, and concentration. Limit the balance, enable strong security, and prove withdrawals early.

## DEX strengths and risks

DEXs allow wallet-to-contract swaps, access to on-chain assets, and self-custody before and after the trade. They can operate without a traditional account. The user pays gas and interacts with contracts directly.

Risks include fake tokens, malicious frontends, unlimited approvals, MEV and poor execution, bridge risk, smart-contract bugs, and irreversible signing. A transaction can be technically successful and economically disastrous.

## Cost comparison

A CEX trade can include deposit fees, trading fee, spread, and withdrawal. A DEX trade can include bridge costs, gas, liquidity-provider fee, price impact, slippage, and wallet-to-fiat exit costs. Compare the complete route.

Small on-chain swaps can be dominated by gas; large illiquid swaps can suffer price impact. A CEX may be cheaper on one asset and worse on another.

## A beginner-friendly hybrid workflow

1. Use WhiteBIT or another vetted CEX for verified fiat onboarding.
2. Buy a liquid asset using a small amount.
3. Withdraw to a wallet on the correct network.
4. Learn to verify token contracts and revoke approvals.
5. Use a DEX only with a separate low-value hot wallet.
6. Keep long-term funds in a wallet that does not connect to random apps.

## Questions to ask before a DEX swap

Where did the frontend URL come from? Is the token contract verified through an authoritative source? What permission will the wallet sign? What is the price impact? Is a bridge required? Can the hardware wallet display enough transaction detail to understand the action?

If the wallet prompt is unreadable or the token exists only through a direct-message link, the correct action is to stop.

## FAQ

### Is a DEX safer than a CEX?

It removes centralized custody during the swap but adds self-custody, contract, approval, and frontend risks. Safety depends on the threat model and user skill.

### Can I use WhiteBIT and a DEX together?

Yes. WhiteBIT can handle fiat onboarding and liquid spot trading; a separate self-custody wallet can handle carefully verified on-chain activity.
