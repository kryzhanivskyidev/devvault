## Start with your one-sentence use case

“I want to buy BTC with EUR once a month and move it to a hardware wallet” is a useful requirement. “I want the best exchange” is not. Your sentence should include starting currency, frequency, assets, approximate size, and final destination.

Once the job is defined, most marketing features disappear. A beginner does not need 500 markets if only two pairs matter, and a trader does not benefit from a beautiful card product if order-book depth is the real constraint.

## The eight-factor scorecard

Score each platform from 1 to 5 and apply a weight:

| Factor | Suggested beginner weight |
|---|---:|
| Legal entity and country availability | 20% |
| Account security controls | 15% |
| EUR funding route | 15% |
| Total cost | 15% |
| Withdrawal ease and networks | 15% |
| Liquidity for needed pairs | 10% |
| Support and recovery | 5% |
| Records and exports | 5% |

Change the weights only when your workflow justifies it. The exercise forces trade-offs into the open.

## Binance: breadth and liquidity

Binance is often strongest when market selection, liquidity, and ecosystem depth matter. That scale can produce competitive execution on major pairs and access to many products. It also creates a busier interface and more opportunities for beginners to wander into leverage or complex yield products.

Compare the local Binance entity and product availability, not a global review. European access and payment methods can vary by country.

## Bybit: active-trading orientation

Bybit deserves attention from users who genuinely need an active-trading environment. Advanced order types and derivatives depth can be meaningful to experienced traders. For beginners, those same features are not a benefit if they encourage leverage before risk management exists.

If the goal is a monthly spot purchase and withdrawal, judge Bybit on that path—not on the sophistication of screens you will not use.

## The small-money bake-off

Open no more than two candidates at once. Secure both accounts properly, fund each with the same small amount, buy the same liquid asset, and withdraw to the same destination network. Record the net amount received, time spent, fee breakdown, and any confusion.

This one exercise tests far more than a table of features. It reveals onboarding friction, payment rails, execution, withdrawal rules, and whether you can reproduce the process calmly.

## The custody decision is separate

Choosing an exchange does not answer where long-term assets should live. An exchange account is convenient and recoverable through identity checks; self-custody removes counterparty custody but makes recovery your responsibility.

Decide the maximum exchange balance and the conditions that trigger a move to a hardware wallet. A good exchange comparison includes the cost and reliability of that exit.

## FAQ

### Which crypto exchange is best for a beginner in Europe?
