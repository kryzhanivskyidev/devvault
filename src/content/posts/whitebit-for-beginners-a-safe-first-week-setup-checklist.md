# WhiteBIT for Beginners: A Safe First-Week Setup Checklist

The riskiest moment on a new crypto exchange is often the first hour. People arrive excited, reuse a familiar password, deposit too much, and only discover the withdrawal screen after the money is already there. A safer approach is intentionally boring: secure first, verify second, test third, scale last.

## Day 1: create the account, then stop

Open the partner link, verify the domain, and register with an email address you control. Use a password manager to generate a unique password. Before depositing, enable either a passkey or authenticator-app 2FA, set the anti-phishing code for official emails, and review device/session controls.

Secure the email account separately. If an attacker can reset your email, the exchange password is not the last line of defense. Save recovery codes offline—not in the same cloud notes account protected by the password they could replace.

## Day 2: complete KYC without improvising

WhiteBIT requires identity verification for deposits, withdrawals, and the main buy functions. Use a valid, readable document and enter your legal details exactly as they appear. EU users may see a different verification path and service set through WhiteBIT EU; follow the entity and country flow shown on the official platform.

Ignore anyone offering paid “verification help.” Do not send identity documents through chat, email replies, or screen-sharing sessions. The only safe place for the process is the official verification interface.

## Day 3: map the money route

Before moving funds, write the route in one line: “EUR bank transfer → WhiteBIT → BTC spot purchase → BTC hardware wallet,” for example. Then check every cost on that exact path. Card funding may be faster but can cost more; bank transfers may be cheaper but slower; crypto deposits introduce a network-matching risk.

Choose one route, identify minimums and limits, and make the first deposit small enough that a mistake is educational rather than painful.

## Day 4: make one simple trade

Beginners do not need margin or futures to learn an exchange. Start with Convert if simplicity matters most, or use Spot to understand a market order versus a limit order. A market order prioritizes execution and can produce slippage; a limit order prioritizes price but may not fill.

Record the amount deposited, amount received, trading fee, and final balance. This tiny ledger shows whether the real cost matched the number you expected.

## Day 5: test the exit

Set up a receiving wallet and confirm that it supports both the asset and the exact network selected on WhiteBIT. Copy the address, compare the first and last characters, and check whether the destination requires a memo or tag. Send the minimum sensible test amount.

Wait for confirmation and verify the transaction in a blockchain explorer. Only after the test arrives should you consider a larger transfer. Never change the address between test and main transfer without repeating the verification.

## Days 6–7: decide your rules

After the round trip, define limits: maximum exchange balance, approved deposit method, approved withdrawal addresses, and how often you will review sessions. Decide which assets are trading capital and which belong in long-term self-custody.

This is also the time to decide that some features are unnecessary. A smaller attack surface is a feature. If you do not use API keys, leverage, or third-party account connections, do not enable them.

## Beginner mistakes that cost real money

- Sending the correct asset over an unsupported network.
- Depositing before enabling 2FA and anti-phishing.
- Trusting a copied address without checking it on the destination device.
- Assuming a stablecoin is risk-free because its price is designed to be stable.
- Keeping recovery codes, seed phrases, and exchange passwords in one cloud folder.
- Treating the first successful trade as proof that withdrawals will be easy.

The first week is successful when you can explain the complete flow and recover access—not when you place the most trades.

## FAQ

### How much should a beginner deposit?

Only an amount small enough to lose without financial harm. The purpose of the first deposit is to test the workflow, not maximize exposure.

### Do I need a hardware wallet immediately?

Not for a tiny learning balance. It becomes increasingly sensible when the value is large enough that device-backed self-custody and offline recovery are worth the responsibility.
