# Exchange Wallet vs Self-Custody: How to Split a Crypto Portfolio by Purpose

The custody debate becomes clearer when the portfolio is divided by purpose. Trading capital needs liquidity. Spending funds need convenience. On-chain experiments need isolation. Long-term reserves need strong key protection and recovery.

WhiteBIT can hold the exchange portion while it is actively used. A hot wallet can handle daily on-chain activity. A hardware wallet can protect long-term assets. The percentages are personal; the roles are universal.

> **Quick verdict:** Use the exchange for near-term transactions, a hot wallet for limited on-chain activity, and hardware-backed self-custody for long-term reserves you can recover safely.
>
> **Best for:** users whose crypto is spread across platforms without a clear reason.

## Bucket 1: exchange working capital

Keep only the amount needed for planned trades, conversions, or near-term fiat access on WhiteBIT or another exchange. The time horizon might be days or weeks. Set a maximum balance and review it regularly.

This bucket accepts counterparty custody in exchange for liquidity and account recovery. Strong login and withdrawal controls remain essential.

## Bucket 2: payments and dApps

A hot wallet holds small amounts for payments, swaps, games, or protocols. It may sign complex approvals and connect to many frontends, so it should not contain the long-term reserve.

Use separate wallet accounts for higher-risk experiments and revoke approvals after use. Treat the balance as exposed working cash.

## Bucket 3: long-term reserve

Use a hardware wallet or carefully designed multi-signature setup for assets that do not need frequent movement. Protect the seed phrase offline, verify addresses on-device, and test recovery before meaningful value arrives.

The reserve should rarely connect to dApps. If a long-term asset must interact with a protocol, consider a separate signing account rather than using the vault directly.

## How to set the split

Estimate one month of exchange activity and one month of wallet spending, then place the remainder according to your custody capability and risk tolerance. The exact percentage changes with trading frequency, portfolio size, and recovery confidence.

If self-custody recovery is untested, improve the process before moving the entire reserve. A theoretical security gain can become a practical loss.

## Rebalancing triggers

Move funds when the exchange balance exceeds its cap, a planned trade ends, the hot wallet grows beyond its spending role, or the threat model changes. Revisit the split after travel, device loss, new beneficiaries, or large portfolio growth.

Do not rebalance during panic. Predefine the trigger and destination while systems are working normally.

## The recovery trade-off

Exchange accounts can offer identity-based recovery but expose users to platform custody and access decisions. Self-custody removes that dependence but can make a lost seed phrase final. Neither model is automatically safer for every person.

Choose the model whose failure modes you can manage. For life-changing amounts, professional custody or multisignature may be more appropriate than one consumer device.

## FAQ

### What percentage should stay on an exchange?

Only the amount with a near-term exchange purpose. Define the cap in money and time rather than copying someone else's percentage.

### Is self-custody always better?

It removes exchange custody risk but adds key-management and recovery risk. It is better only when operated correctly.
