# A Low-Maintenance Crypto Security Routine for Busy People

Most crypto security advice is a giant checklist performed once. Real protection comes from a small routine that survives busy weeks, travel, device upgrades, and changing balances. The objective is not maximum complexity; it is consistent detection and recovery.

This schedule covers a WhiteBIT account, email, hot wallet, hardware wallet, and the records connecting them. It takes minutes most weeks and becomes deeper only when value or risk changes.

> **Quick verdict:** A short recurring routine beats a perfect setup that is never reviewed. Automate reminders, not irreversible transactions.
>
> **Best for:** people who use crypto occasionally and want sustainable security without daily monitoring.
>
> **Look elsewhere if:** high-frequency traders or organizations that need continuous monitoring and formal controls.

## Every week: five minutes

Review recent exchange and wallet activity, confirm there are no unexpected withdrawals or logins, and check high-value balances against your limits. Install operating-system and browser security updates from official channels.

Do not update hardware-wallet firmware simply because an email says so. Open the official companion application and verify the release there.

## Every month: accounts and permissions

Review WhiteBIT sessions and devices, email forwarding rules, API keys, withdrawal-address entries, connected wallet sites, and token allowances. Revoke anything unused. Export transaction history and store it with financial records.

Move exchange or hot-wallet balances that exceed their purpose-defined caps.

## Every quarter: recovery

Confirm that offline recovery codes and seed backups remain accessible and undamaged. Use supported non-destructive tools to verify the hardware-wallet backup. Review the incident plan and official support links.

Do not gather every secret in one room for the review. Independence is part of the design.

## Every year: simplify

Close unused exchange accounts, remove abandoned wallets from daily devices, delete obsolete API integrations, rotate exposed credentials, and update beneficiaries or inheritance instructions. Review whether the portfolio value now requires multisignature or professional custody.

Complexity accumulates quietly. Annual cleanup reduces the number of things that can be forgotten or phished.

## Event-driven checks

Run an immediate review after a lost device, SIM change, travel, suspicious email, password-manager breach notice, new wallet, large portfolio increase, or change in who needs recovery access. Events change the threat model faster than the calendar.

Security changes can trigger withdrawal holds, so schedule them before a large transfer rather than during one.

## What to automate

Automate calendar reminders, balance alerts, login notifications, transaction exports, and read-only monitoring. Avoid unattended withdrawals and scripts with broad permissions. Alerts should lead to a human decision.

Keep the routine on one page. If it requires a manual to perform, it will not survive a busy month.

## FAQ

### How often should I review exchange sessions?

Monthly is a reasonable baseline and immediately after travel, device loss, or suspicious activity.

### Should I rotate passwords on a schedule?

Unique, uncompromised passwords do not need arbitrary rotation. Change them after exposure, reuse discovery, or a relevant incident.

The routine should be shorter than the consequences it prevents. Watch activity weekly, remove permissions monthly, test recovery quarterly, and simplify annually. Add event-driven reviews when the threat model changes.
