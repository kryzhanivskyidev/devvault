# Crypto Travel Security: How to Protect Accounts, Wallets, and Devices Abroad

A security setup that feels solid at home can become fragile in transit. Airports, hotels, coworking spaces, border checks, SIM changes, shoulder surfing, and theft all increase exposure. The worst time to design recovery is after the phone with your email, authenticator, and wallet disappears.

The goal is not to carry maximum security equipment. It is to minimize what travels, preserve a safe recovery path, and avoid turning one bag into the key to every account.

> **Quick verdict:** Travel with less value, fewer credentials, and a rehearsed recovery plan. Network privacy helps, but device and account separation matter more.
>
> **Best for:** remote workers, frequent travelers, and anyone who may access WhiteBIT or a wallet away from home.
>
> **Look elsewhere if:** people planning to carry a hardware wallet, seed backup, laptop, and recovery codes together.

## One week before departure

Update the operating system and wallet apps through official stores, review exchange sessions, remove unused API keys, and test backups. Decide which accounts must be accessible on the trip. Move excess exchange and hot-wallet balances to a protected long-term setup.

Save official support links and transaction records in an encrypted location. Tell a trusted person how to help without giving them the seed phrase.

## Build a travel wallet

Create a separate wallet containing only the amount needed for the trip. Do not connect the long-term vault to hotel Wi-Fi or unfamiliar dApps. If carrying a hardware wallet, keep its recovery phrase at home in a separate secure location.

Consider whether the device is necessary at all. The safest travel hardware can be hardware left behind.

## Harden the phone and laptop

Use strong screen locks, full-disk encryption, remote-wipe capability, and minimal lock-screen notifications. Disable automatic connections to open Wi-Fi and remove saved networks. Keep browsers and password managers updated.

Do not store screenshots of seed phrases, KYC documents, or recovery codes in the photo library. Cloud syncing can copy the exposure to multiple devices.

## Networks: what a VPN does and does not do

A reputable VPN encrypts traffic between the device and VPN server, which is useful on untrusted networks. It does not verify a phishing domain, clean malware, or make a malicious wallet approval safe.

Prefer mobile data or a trusted personal hotspot for sensitive activity. If a login is not urgent, wait. Travel is a poor time for large transfers or security-setting changes.

## At borders and shared spaces

Laws and device-search powers vary. Understand the legal environment before traveling and seek advice for high-risk jurisdictions. Minimize locally stored sensitive data and log out of accounts that do not need to travel.

Use privacy screens and avoid discussing holdings in public. Physical coercion is part of the threat model when visible balances or wallet devices advertise value.

## If a device is lost

From a trusted device, lock or wipe the missing hardware, revoke email and exchange sessions, change relevant passwords, and notify the mobile provider. If a wallet seed may be exposed, create a new wallet on a clean device and move assets.

Do not use search ads to find support during a crisis. Navigate from a saved official link and never share a seed phrase or authenticator code.

## FAQ

### Should I travel with a hardware wallet?

Only if needed. Never carry it together with the seed backup, and keep the visible balance appropriate for the trip.

### Is hotel Wi-Fi safe with a VPN?

A VPN improves transport privacy but cannot fix a compromised device or phishing site. Use a trusted hotspot for sensitive actions when possible.
