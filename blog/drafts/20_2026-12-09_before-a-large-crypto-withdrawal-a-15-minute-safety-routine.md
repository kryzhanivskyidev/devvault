# Before a Large Crypto Withdrawal: A 15-Minute Safety Routine

The size of a crypto withdrawal should change the process. An address copied correctly yesterday can be wrong today. The network can be under maintenance. A destination can require a memo. A recent password change can trigger a hold. Urgency magnifies every hidden assumption.

Use this routine for WhiteBIT or any exchange. It is designed to slow the decision just enough to catch errors without turning a transfer into a day-long ceremony.

> **Quick verdict:** Never send a large amount as the first transaction on a route. Verify the environment, run a test, then repeat the checks for the main transfer.
>
> **Best for:** users withdrawing an amount whose loss would materially hurt.
>
> **Look elsewhere if:** anyone acting under pressure from a stranger, support impersonator, investment group, or emergency message.

## Minute 0–2: remove pressure

Stop conversations, screen sharing, and background tasks. Confirm that the withdrawal was your idea. No legitimate support agent needs you to move funds to “verify” or “protect” an account.

If another person is directing the timing, destination, or network, pause the transaction and verify independently.

## Minute 2–4: secure the environment

Use a patched personal device and a trusted network. Navigate through a saved bookmark or known partner link, verify the domain, and check for unexpected sessions or security changes. Close remote-access software.

For a hardware wallet, unlock it privately and confirm that its companion application is official.

## Minute 4–6: check platform status and limits

Review WhiteBIT's system page or the exchange's status page for the asset and network. Check minimums, fees, account limits, and any temporary withdrawal lock after password or 2FA changes.

Do not use an alternate network because the intended one is temporarily unavailable unless the destination explicitly supports it.

## Minute 6–9: verify the destination

Generate the address from the receiving wallet. On a hardware wallet, confirm it on the device screen. On another exchange, check the deposit network and memo/tag. Compare the pasted address character by character at the beginning, middle, and end.

Treat address-book entries and transaction history as hints, not proof. Address poisoning can create convincing look-alikes.

## Minute 9–11: calculate the result

Confirm asset, network, amount, fee, and final received amount. Check whether the destination has a minimum deposit. Consider tax or reporting implications and save the purpose of the transfer.

For a new route, the first amount should be small enough to lose without harm and large enough to clear all minimums.

## Minute 11–14: send the test

Submit the test, save the transaction ID, and observe it in the correct explorer. Wait for the destination to credit it. Do not rely only on the exchange's “completed” label.

If the test is delayed, investigate. Do not send the main amount to “push it through.”

## Minute 14–15: repeat before the main transfer

Reopen or re-check the destination address, asset, network, and memo. Confirm the test arrived in the exact intended account. Then submit the main transaction and save its identifier.

For very large amounts, split the transfer or use a second trusted reviewer. A second person should verify facts, not receive the seed phrase.

## FAQ

### How large is a large withdrawal?

Any amount whose loss would materially affect you. The threshold is personal, so the process should scale with consequence.

### Can I skip the test to save a fee?

The test fee is the cost of validating the entire route. Skipping it is rarely rational for a meaningful amount.
