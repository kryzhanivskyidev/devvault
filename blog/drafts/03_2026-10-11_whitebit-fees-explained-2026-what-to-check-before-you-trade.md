## The only fee formula you need

Use this model:

**Total cost = funding fee + trading fee + spread + slippage + withdrawal fee + network or bank costs.**

Calculate the result in both currency and percentage terms. If you deposit €500 and only €485 of value reaches your external wallet, the real cost is €15 or 3%—even if the trading screen charged a fraction of one percent.

This formula also prevents bad comparisons. Exchange A can have a lower spot fee and still be more expensive for your preferred card, bank, asset, or network route.

## Maker versus taker on WhiteBIT

A maker order adds liquidity to the order book and waits to be matched. A taker order removes existing liquidity and executes against available orders. WhiteBIT's live page currently shows standard spot maker and taker pricing, while some pairs and account tiers can differ.

The distinction matters most for frequent traders. A beginner making one purchase should care just as much about execution quality. A limit order placed far from the market may never fill; a market order can fill immediately but cross the spread and slip in a thin market. The fee label does not describe the entire execution.

## Convert versus Spot

Convert is designed for speed and simplicity. Spot exposes the order book, order type, and explicit trade mechanics. The cheaper choice depends on the quote, liquidity, and how carefully the spot order is placed.

Compare the final amount, not the interface. Preview the Convert quote, then inspect the spot market at the same time. If the difference is trivial for a small purchase, simplicity may win. If you trade often or in size, understanding the order book becomes more valuable.

## Funding costs can dominate

The route into the exchange often costs more than the trade itself. Bank transfer, card purchase, third-party payment provider, and crypto deposit each have different economics and risks. A card can include provider fees and foreign-exchange costs; a bank transfer can have minimums or timing constraints; a crypto deposit can be cheap on one network and unrecoverable on the wrong one.

Price the route that is actually available in your country and account tier. Do not assume another user's screenshot applies to your entity, currency, or payment provider.

## A worked example

Imagine a €1,000 deposit. The funding route costs €10. The spot fee is 0.1%, or roughly €0.99 after funding. Spread and slippage cost another €2. The withdrawal fee is equivalent to €4. Your approximate total cost is €16.99, or 1.70% of the original deposit.

Now change only the funding route to a low-cost bank transfer and the total can fall sharply. Or choose an illiquid pair and slippage can overwhelm every advertised saving. The lesson is not that WhiteBIT is expensive or cheap; it is that your route determines the answer.

## When fee discounts are worth caring about

Volume tiers and ecosystem-token benefits can reduce certain fees, but they introduce another decision: are you taking additional asset exposure or locking capital to save on trading? Estimate the annual saving first. If a discount saves €20 but requires you to hold an asset whose price can move by much more, it is not a free discount.

Frequent traders should model monthly volume, maker/taker mix, and financing costs. Occasional buyers should optimize funding, liquidity, and withdrawal simplicity before chasing tiers.
