## Trading fee

The maker fee applies when an order rests and adds liquidity; the taker fee applies when an order executes against existing liquidity. Your order type and fill determine which one applies. Some exchanges change rates by pair, volume, membership, or token holdings.

Do not assume a limit order is automatically a maker order. A limit order that crosses the current book can execute immediately as taker.

## Spread and slippage

The spread is the gap between the best buy and sell prices. Slippage is the difference between the expected and actual execution as an order moves through available liquidity. Both can exceed the explicit fee on thin markets.

Inspect order-book depth at your intended size. A market that looks liquid for €100 can behave differently for €20,000.

## Funding and conversion

Cards, bank transfers, third-party providers, and crypto deposits have different costs. Your bank can add foreign-exchange or transfer fees that the exchange does not control. A “free deposit” can still cost network fees on the sending side.

If your starting currency is not supported directly, include the conversion spread before the crypto trade.

## Withdrawal and network costs

Exchanges commonly publish a fixed or dynamic withdrawal fee by asset and network. Compare the amount the destination receives and check minimums. A cheap network is not useful if the destination does not support it.

For frequent small withdrawals, this line can dominate. Batch responsibly or choose another supported network only after compatibility is confirmed.

## Leverage costs

Margin and derivatives add borrowing rates, funding payments, liquidation fees or losses, and a larger behavioural risk. A low maker fee cannot compensate for poor leverage control. Compare these products separately from spot.

Beginners should not use futures pricing as evidence that an exchange is cheap for a simple spot purchase.

## Discounts and tiers

VIP tiers and ecosystem-token discounts can lower trading costs. Calculate the annual saving and compare it with capital requirements, token volatility, and added complexity. A discount that changes your asset exposure is not free.

Use the rate you actually qualify for today, not the maximum advertised discount.

## The comparison worksheet

For each exchange, enter: starting fiat amount, funding fee, effective trade price, trading fee, spread/slippage estimate, withdrawal fee, network cost, and final wallet value. Divide total loss from the starting amount by the starting amount.

Run three sizes—small, normal, and large—because fixed withdrawal fees and slippage change the result. The cheapest platform can switch as size changes.

## FAQ

### Which fee matters most?

The largest fee on your route. For small purchases it may be funding or withdrawal; for active trading it may be execution and maker/taker costs.

### Are zero-fee trades free?

No. Spread, conversion, funding, withdrawal, and network costs can remain even when the explicit trading fee is zero.
